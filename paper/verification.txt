Formulation, host, scattering and Galactic-orbit manuscript verification
Revision 0.8.0-manuscript.1; 25 October 2026

Completed calculations
One actual NGC 3201 joint posterior sample is reconstructed with pinned,
historically compatible model implementations. Sixteen mass species supply
one consistent potential, density and local distribution-function family.
Twenty local states evaluate anisotropic drift and diffusion per unit constant
Coulomb logarithm. The 60-solar-mass remnant is a diagnostic probe.
Original fit-run software hashes are unknown; exact historical fit reproduction
and new observational fitting are not claimed.
Four static boundary scenarios and four Galactic reference orbits evaluate
external-force and host-support diagnostics. The stellar host is held fixed
for these diagnostics, not evolved or re-equilibrated over the orbital horizon.

Numerical checks
- Mass, half-mass radius, force, integrated potential and virial balance pass
  the stated numerical tolerances.
- Independent velocity quadrature recovers all-species density and raw moments.
- Independent projection checks the luminous tracer, with the original
  projected posterior envelopes retained as descriptive comparisons.
- Analytic uniform-ball kernels, mass/time factors, anisotropic rotational
  covariance, tensor trace identity, PSD and quadrature refinement pass.
- A separate one-dimensional central integral verifies the host's diffusion
  trace. Exterior test velocities remain outside the collision API's validated
  domain rather than silently accepted by insufficient quadrature.
- Seventeen numerical tests pass in the recorded host environment. They cover
  host consistency, collision kernels, static boundary limits, Galactic
  potential gradients and force derivatives, analytic spatial force bounds,
  coordinate orientation, distance transformations, orbit conservation and
  reversal, and the rotating-frame angular-acceleration convention.
- Four static boundary scenarios pass independent density integration and
  radial-mesh refinement checks.
- Galactic orbits pass energy and axial angular-momentum checks. The two
  central cases pass tighter integration and time-reversal checks; the main
  host-distance case also passes finer output sampling and tidal-derivative
  refinement. Catalog orbital extents provide a descriptive scale comparison,
  not an exact reproduction or an empirical validation of the potential.

Scientific limits
Numerical convergence does not validate smooth diffusion from a few heavy
objects. Expected black-hole counts, density and scattering fractions are
conditional model quantities, not observed counts or measured relaxation.
The formal outer truncation radius is not an adopted tidal escape boundary.
Neither the circular logarithmic-field diagnostic nor the eccentric orbit's
frozen-frame radius is an observed outer edge or an escape rule. Exterior
mass is not classified as stripped or escaped.
The analytic Galactic-force bound holds throughout a spatial ball in the
specified potential. Its time-dependent extrema are numerically sampled and
refined. The proposed 8 pc control radius is conditional on this host, orbit,
1 percent force tolerance and horizon; it is not a universal validity radius
or a cumulative trajectory-error guarantee. A forcing-timescale indicator
identifies a host-response question; it is not a heating or mass-loss rate.
The host is not made tidally self-consistent by these calculations. Galactic,
solar-frame and full phase-space covariance have not been propagated. The
four orbit cases are conditional comparisons, not a posterior sample.
No physical impact-parameter range, calibrated discrete-encounter partition,
merger-conditioned launch law, remnant trajectory ensemble or residence
distribution has been evaluated. The recorded results identify the physical
questions to resolve before that evolution can be interpreted.
