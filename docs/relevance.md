# Scientific relevance and evidence standard

Recoil can change how a merger remnant samples a cluster while remaining bound. Its initial state, host structure, scattering, boundaries, and available physical time determine that motion.

## Step 1: transport as a conditional prediction

The transport problem asks for position–velocity distributions conditional on a merger and host. It preserves off-center launches, progenitor motion, anisotropy, species masses, friction, and random deflections. More terms do not automatically make a model more accurate: each requires a consistent input and applicability test.

Local drift/diffusion theory is established physics. [Tep, Fouvry & Pichon (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03985) treats anisotropic gravitational relaxation. [Webb et al. (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09100) identifies limitations of simplified friction for kicked black holes. These support testing the accuracy and domain of a reduced transport description; they do not establish a new scattering mechanism.

## Step 2: environmental observables

Residence, first entry, re-entry, and density exposure answer different questions. Their definitions must retain the finite window, moving-region convention, and reasons histories end. Survival-weighted identities connect trajectory averages to the transport density without discarding stopped paths.

The objective is to establish which physical information preserves these observables at a declared accuracy. A comparison must control the host, launch ensemble, horizon, and observable definition. More parameter permutations alone do not establish improved physical understanding.

## Evidence required for a conclusion

Mathematical consistency defines the calculation. Numerical convergence and independent checks assess implementation accuracy. Independently resolved dynamics are needed to assess physical adequacy. Observational claims additionally require a justified mapping from data to the conditional host and merger population.

The present scope supplies definitions and acceptance requirements. It supplies no calibrated predictions, observational constraints, or discovery claim, and no companion-acquisition or repeated-merger rate.
